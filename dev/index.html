DominguezGarcia2019.jl

A reproduction of Domínguez-García, Dakos & Kéfi (2019), "Unveiling dimensions of stability in complex ecological networks", PNAS 116:25714 (doi:10.1073/pnas.1904470116).

The paper computes 27 stability metrics on simulated niche-model food webs, correlates them within each species richness, and groups them into three "dimensions" of stability. This package reproduces it end to end, from the simulator, which is not public and was rebuilt from the SI, to the published groups, and then asks how far the conclusions carry.

It is a case study. It holds only what is specific to the paper, and takes everything else from the ecorecipes packages:

PackageProvides
ResilienceCore.jlthe metrics (resilience, reactivity, amplification, median metrics, invariability, sensitivities) and the press and extinction experiments; re-exported here
FoodWebDynamics.jlthe niche model, the allometric bioenergetic model, community assembly and the GATEWAy reader
CommunityMatrices.jlinteraction matrices, structural stability and sign patterns, which began in this package
DynamicalResilience.jlresilience of SciML models: steady states, thresholds, recovery and basin stability

What is here:

  • Conventions: community_record computes the 39 raw columns of Dataset S1 for an assembled community. Two of them follow what the published data did rather than what the SI says: the ∞-norm for Amax and tmax, and misaligned pulse weights for the median metrics. Both can be switched to the SI's definitions.
  • Dataset transforms: prepare_metrics and read_metrics turn raw columns into the paper's 27 "larger is more stable" metrics, as the authors' code does.
  • Analysis: per-size Spearman correlations, size-class averages, networks of metrics and their modular groups, hierarchical clustering, explained variance and covariance ellipsoids. They are checked against the authors' Python code.
  • Data: the authors' Dataset S2, a 10,000-community ensemble simulated with the reconstructed model, and ensembles under alternative model settings.

The tutorials reproduce the analysis on the authors' data, follow one food web through all 27 metrics, compare a simulated ensemble with the published one, show that the "early response to pulse" dimension is an indexing artefact, run the pipeline on observed food webs, and test the conclusions against changes to the model's settings.

DominguezGarcia2019

A reproduction of Domínguez-García, Dakos & Kéfi (2019), "Unveiling dimensions of stability in complex ecological networks", PNAS 116:25714 (doi:10.1073/pnas.1904470116).

This is a case study: it holds only what is specific to the paper.

  • community_record computes the 39 raw columns of the paper's Dataset S1 for an assembled food web, with the conventions the published data actually used (amplification_metrics, published_pulse_covariance, published_max_amplification).
  • prepare_metrics and read_metrics turn raw columns into the paper's 27 "larger is more stable" metrics.
  • The analysis functions (size_correlations, modular_groups, explained_variance, …) port the authors' Python code.

The metrics themselves come from ResilienceCore.jl, on a ContinuousJacobian (see community_jacobian), and are re-exported. The food webs, the bioenergetic model and community assembly come from FoodWebDynamics.jl. Interaction matrices, structural stability and sign patterns are in CommunityMatrices.jl.
