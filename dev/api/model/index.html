Food webs and the bioenergetic model

EcologicalResilience.FoodWeb — Type
FoodWeb(A::AbstractMatrix{Bool})

A directed feeding network on S = size(A, 1) species, with A[i, j] == true when species i eats species j (rows are consumers, columns are resources). Basal species (producers) are those with no resources. Construct one directly or with niche_model.

source
EcologicalResilience.niche_model — Function
niche_model([rng], S, C)

Draw a food web of S species from the niche model of Williams & Martinez (2000) with expected directed connectance C (0 < C < 1/2). The species with the smallest niche value has a feeding range of zero, so the web has at least one basal species. Species are indexed in increasing order of niche value. The model does not depend on this order, but the published median metrics do (see published_pulse_covariance). No webs are rejected here; trophic_levels returns nothing for webs that a community-assembly step should reject.

EcologicalResilience.trophic_levels — Function
trophic_levels(web::FoodWeb; alive = trues(richness(web)))

Prey-averaged trophic levels among the alive species: 1 for a basal species, and one plus the mean trophic level of its living resources for a consumer. Returns nothing when they are undefined, i.e. when some living consumer has no path to a basal species (for example a closed loop of consumers). Dead species get NaN.

EcologicalResilience.BioenergeticParameters — Type
BioenergeticParameters(; kwargs...)

Constants of the bioenergetic model of Domínguez-García et al. (2019, SI §8). Keywords and defaults:

  • q = 0.3: Hill coefficient (the functional response has exponent 1 + q);
  • Z = 1.5: predator–prey body-mass ratio, mᵢ = Z^(Lᵢ − 1);
  • K = 1.0: plant carrying capacity; plant growth rate is 1;
  • e_plant = 0.45, e_animal = 0.85: assimilation efficiency on plant and animal prey;
  • x_plant = 0.138, x_animal = 0.314: metabolic constants, xᵢ = x mᵢ^metabolic_exponent;
  • metabolic_exponent = -0.25: printed as +0.25 in the SI;
  • d0 = 0.1: natural mortality as a fraction of metabolism, dᵢ = d0 xᵢ;
  • a0 = 50.0, a0_plant = 10.0: capture coefficients for animal and plant prey;
  • h0 = 0.4: handling-time constant (not given in the paper);
  • alpha_consumer = 0.7, alpha_resource = 0.4, eta_consumer = -0.6, eta_resource = 0.5: means of the per-community allometric exponents of capture (α) and handling (η);
  • exponent_cv = 0.1: their standard deviation as a fraction of the absolute mean;
  • extinction_threshold = 1e-6: a species is extinct below extinction_threshold * mᵢ;
  • plant_metabolism = false: whether plants pay the metabolic loss xᵢBᵢ. SI §8 lists a plant metabolic rate, but in Brose et al. (2006) producers have none, and only false reproduces Dataset S1's total biomass per plant (median 0.98). Natural mortality d0 x mᵢ^metabolic_exponent applies to plants either way.
  • renormalise_diet = false: whether a consumer's diet weight wᵢ is reset to 1/(number of living resources) when a resource dies. By default it stays at 1/(number of resources in the initial web). SI §9 only deletes the dead species' links. Keeping wᵢ reproduces Dataset S1's tolerance to extinctions, cascading extinctions and mortality tolerance at large richness (median TE 0.332 against 0.334 published at N 61–99; renormalising gives 0.393).

The defaults of metabolic_exponent, h0 and plant_metabolism were chosen by matching summaries of Dataset S1 (lab/wiki/model/calibration.md).

EcologicalResilience.AllometricExponents — Type
AllometricExponents(alpha_consumer, alpha_resource, eta_consumer, eta_resource)

The exponents of one community: capture aᵢⱼ ∝ mᵢ^alpha_consumer mⱼ^alpha_resource and handling hᵢⱼ ∝ mᵢ^eta_consumer mⱼ^eta_resource. The dataset of Domínguez-García et al. (2019) records them per community as a_i, a_j, h_i and h_j.

EcologicalResilience.FoodWebModel — Type
FoodWebModel(web::FoodWeb, p::BioenergeticParameters, ex::AllometricExponents; mass = nothing)

The bioenergetic model on web, with rates from p and ex. Throws an ArgumentError if the trophic levels of web are undefined (see trophic_levels).

Body masses are Z^(L − 1) from prey-averaged trophic levels L (SI §9), unless mass gives them, e.g. measured masses for an observed web. Measured masses must be relative to the producers' mass, because producers have mass 1 in the model; producers' entries of mass are ignored.

The model is mutable state: alive marks surviving species, mortality holds a multiplier of each species' natural mortality (all ones at baseline) and w the diet weights, 1/(number of resources) (living resources only if params.renormalise_diet). Change them with kill! and set_mortality!. Use rhs! and jacobian! for the vector field and its Jacobian.

jacobian(a::AlphaMatrix; N = equilibrium(a))

The Jacobian of the Lotka–Volterra system a at abundances N: diag(N) A at an interior equilibrium (for other N the general form diag(r + A N) + diag(N) A is used).

source
jacobian(f::FluxMatrix; efficiency, self_regulation)

The Jacobian implied by steady-state fluxes when feeding is mass action, Fᵢⱼ = aᵢⱼ Bᵢ Bⱼ (de Ruiter et al. 1995): the effect of resource j on consumer i is eⱼ Fᵢⱼ / Bⱼ, the effect of consumer j on resource i is −Fⱼᵢ / Bⱼ, and the diagonal is −self_regulation (which fluxes cannot determine). efficiency is a vector over resources (or a scalar); self_regulation a vector (or scalar) of nonnegative rates.

EcologicalResilience.SteadyStateOptions — Type
SteadyStateOptions(; window = 100.0, tolerance = 5e-4, tmax = 1e6, abstol = 1e-10,
                     reltol = 1e-8, polish = true)

How settle! decides that a community is at steady state. The integration proceeds in windows of length window; it stops when every living species' log biomass changed by less than tolerance over the last window (SI §9 uses 0.0005 and leaves the window unstated), or fails at tmax (TSAFE = 10⁶ in SI §9). With polish = true the state is then refined to the equilibrium by Newton's method. Also with polish = true, once the change over a window is below `earlypolish * tolerance, a Newton step is tried, and accepted if it lands on a locally stable equilibrium withinearlypolishdistance(in log biomass) of the current state; this avoids integrating along slow modes for very long times.algis any SciML ODE solver; implicit solvers are given the model's analytic Jacobian.maxitersbounds the number of solver steps of one integration. A run that has not converged stops once no species has gone extinct forstall` time units (default: never); it is then reported as not converged, with its current state (a point on its trajectory, so that further perturbations can continue from it).

source
EcologicalResilience.settle! — Function
settle!(model::FoodWebModel, B0; options = SteadyStateOptions())

Integrate model from biomasses B0 until steady state, killing species that fall below their extinction threshold (which updates model.alive). Returns (B, converged), where B is the full biomass vector (zero for dead species) and converged is false if options.tmax was reached first, the integration failed, or the Newton polish did not land on a locally stable equilibrium with every survivor above its threshold.

source
EcologicalResilience.polish! — Function
polish!(model::FoodWebModel, B; maxiter = 50, tol = 1e-11)

Refine B in place to an equilibrium of the living species by Newton's method. Returns true if it converged to a point where every living species is above its extinction threshold and the Jacobian is locally stable (every eigenvalue has negative real part).

EcologicalResilience.assemble_community — Function
assemble_community([rng], S; params = BioenergeticParameters(), C = 0.15,
                   survival = 0.9, initial_biomass = (0.05, 1.0),
                   options = SteadyStateOptions())

One attempt at the assembly procedure of SI §9: draw a niche web of S species with connectance C and the community's allometric exponents, start from biomasses uniform on initial_biomass, integrate to steady state and remove plants that no living species eats. Returns (community, status). community is nothing and status says why when the attempt is discarded:

  • :trophic_levels: undefined trophic levels (SI: "the network is discarded");
  • :not_converged: no steady state by T_SAFE, or no locally stable equilibrium;
  • :extinctions: fewer than floor(survival * S) species are left once isolated plants are removed;
  • :empty: every survivor was an isolated plant, so nothing is left once they are removed (possible in small webs without consumers).
  • :neutral: the equilibrium has an eigenvalue 0 (to within 10⁻⁹), as when two dynamically identical consumers survive. It is not asymptotically stable, and polish!'s strict < 0 test admits it about half the time because the zero is computed as ±10⁻¹⁷. The published Dataset S1 has none (its smallest Rinf is 1.6×10⁻⁴).

The 90% rule is applied as SI §9 orders it, after isolated plants are removed, to the integer floor(survival * S). Every one of the 19,816 communities of Dataset S1 satisfies N ≥ floor(0.9 iniN) and 5,679 sit exactly at it. The real-valued N ≥ 0.9 iniN would reject those. Applying the rule before the removal would let about 4% of communities fall below the floor; none of the published ones does.
