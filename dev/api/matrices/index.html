Interaction matrices

Typed matrices make explicit which object a number belongs to, and conversions between them ask for what they need.

EcologicalResilience.MatrixMetadata — Type
MatrixMetadata(; taxa = String[], state_variables, units, sign_convention, spatial_bounds,
                 temporal_bounds, derivation, uncertainty, software, licence)

The minimal metadata record proposed for deposited interaction matrices (interaction-matrix review, lab/papers/interaction): the species (taxa, in row order), what the state variables are, units, the sign convention, spatial and temporal bounds, how the entries were derived, how their uncertainty is represented, the software used and the licence. All fields are strings except taxa; unknown fields default to "unspecified".

EcologicalResilience.InteractionMatrix — Type
InteractionMatrix

Supertype of the typed interaction matrices: AlphaMatrix, Jacobian, CommunityMatrix, FluxMatrix, SignedDigraph and ProbabilisticNetwork. Use matrix for the numbers, matrix_metadata and taxa for the record, and size/nspecies for the dimension.

EcologicalResilience.AlphaMatrix — Type
AlphaMatrix(A, r; meta = MatrixMetadata())

Generalised Lotka–Volterra coefficients: dNᵢ/dt = Nᵢ (rᵢ + Σⱼ Aᵢⱼ Nⱼ), with per-capita effects A (negative diagonal for self-regulation) and intrinsic growth rates r. Other parameterisations (e.g. annual-plant λᵢ/(1 + Σⱼ αᵢⱼ Nⱼ)) must be converted first; the conversion belongs in meta.derivation.

source

glv_rhs!(du, N, a::AlphaMatrix, t)

The generalised Lotka–Volterra vector field of a in the in-place SciML form.

source
EcologicalResilience.equilibrium — Function
equilibrium(a::AlphaMatrix)

The interior equilibrium N* = −A⁻¹ r (all species present), which may have negative entries.

EcologicalResilience.isfeasible — Function
isfeasible(a::AlphaMatrix)

Whether the interior equilibrium of a has every species at positive abundance.

EcologicalResilience.Jacobian — Type
Jacobian(J, equilibrium = Float64[]; meta = MatrixMetadata())
Jacobian(c::Community)

The Jacobian (population-level community matrix) of a dynamical system at an equilibrium, with that equilibrium when known. All Jacobian-based metrics accept it; the median metrics then use pulses proportional to equilibrium.

source
EcologicalResilience.CommunityMatrix — Type
CommunityMatrix(M, equilibrium; meta = MatrixMetadata())

Per-capita effects at an equilibrium: Mᵢⱼ = ∂(Ṅᵢ/Nᵢ)/∂Nⱼ. For Lotka–Volterra it is the coefficient matrix A. Convert with jacobian(m) = diag(N*) M and per_capita(J).

source
EcologicalResilience.per_capita — Function
per_capita(j::Jacobian)

The per-capita CommunityMatrix diag(N*)⁻¹ J; needs the equilibrium, and every species present.

source
EcologicalResilience.FluxMatrix — Type
FluxMatrix(F, biomass; meta = MatrixMetadata())

Feeding fluxes at a steady state: F[i, j] is the rate at which consumer i removes biomass of resource j (e.g. from Ecopath or fluxweb), with the standing biomass of every species. jacobian turns it into a Jacobian under stated assumptions.

source
EcologicalResilience.SignedDigraph — Type
SignedDigraph(S; meta = MatrixMetadata())
SignedDigraph(x::InteractionMatrix)

A sign pattern (entries −1, 0, +1) of the effects of species on each other, as used in loop analysis and qualitative press-perturbation analysis. The second form takes the signs of the Jacobian or community matrix of x.

source
EcologicalResilience.ProbabilisticNetwork — Type
ProbabilisticNetwork(P; meta = MatrixMetadata())

Probabilities P[i, j] that species j affects species i (for example, that i eats j), e.g. from sampling-corrected or predicted links. rand(rng, p) draws a BitMatrix of links.

source
EcologicalResilience.jacobian_metrics — Function
jacobian_metrics(j::Jacobian)

The Jacobian-based stability metrics of Domínguez-García et al. (2019) for any Jacobian, in the raw sign convention of their dataset: Rinf, R0, Is, Amax, tmax, S (community sensitivity) and absumS, and, when the equilibrium is known, MRinf, MR0, MAmax, Mtmax.

invasion_rates(a::AlphaMatrix, N)

source
EcologicalResilience.steady_state — Function
steady_state(a::AlphaMatrix, N0; pool = findall(>(0), N0), tmax = 1e4, threshold = 1e-8,
             reseed = 1e-6, rounds = 20)

The assembled state of the Lotka–Volterra system a from N0: integrate to tmax, remove species below threshold, and solve for the survivors' interior equilibrium; while some species of the regional pool (by default those present in N0) could invade (positive invasion rate), reintroduce them at density reseed and repeat (up to rounds times). Returns (N, survivors, stable, uninvadable): the equilibrium (zeros for absent species), the survivors' indices, whether their Jacobian is stable, and whether every absent species of the pool has a negative invasion rate.

source
EcologicalResilience.glv_trajectory — Function
glv_trajectory(a::AlphaMatrix, N0, times; forcing = nothing, susceptibility = nothing,
               tstops = Float64[])

Abundances of the Lotka–Volterra system a at times from N0 at times[1], optionally with a perturbation that adds susceptibility[i] × forcing(t) to species i's per-capita growth rate (the antibiotic model of Stein et al. 2013); give the forcing's switching times as tstops. Returns a matrix with one row per time.

Structural stability and sign patterns

EcologicalResilience.feasibility_domain — Function
feasibility_domain([rng], a::AlphaMatrix; samples = 100_000)

The size of the feasibility domain of a (Saavedra et al. 2017): the fraction Ω of directions of the growth-rate vector r (uniform on the unit sphere) for which the interior equilibrium −A⁻¹ r is positive, estimated from samples random directions. Returns (Ω, Ω^(1/S), se), with se the Monte Carlo standard error of Ω. Ω^(1/S) is comparable across community sizes; for A = −I it is 1/2.

source
EcologicalResilience.structural_vector — Function
structural_vector(a::AlphaMatrix)

The centroid of the feasibility cone of a: the normalised sum of the unit columns of −A (Saavedra et al. 2017). Growth-rate vectors along it are the most robustly feasible.

source
EcologicalResilience.feasibility_angle — Function
feasibility_angle(a::AlphaMatrix)

The angle in degrees between the growth-rate vector a.r and the structural_vector of a: a measure of how far the community is from the centre of its feasibility domain (larger means closer to losing feasibility).

source
EcologicalResilience.quasi_sign_stability — Function
quasi_sign_stability([rng], s::SignedDigraph; samples = 10_000)

The fraction of matrices with sign pattern s and magnitudes uniform on (0, 1) that are stable (every eigenvalue with negative real part): quasi-sign-stability (Allesina & Pascual 2008). A diagonal of zeros means no self-regulation. Returns (fraction, se).

source
EcologicalResilience.press_sign_determinacy — Function
press_sign_determinacy([rng], s::SignedDigraph; samples = 10_000)

Qualitative press-perturbation analysis (Dambacher et al. 2003; the simulation approach of QPress): draw matrices with sign pattern s and magnitudes uniform on (0, 1), keep the stable ones, and for each compute the equilibrium response to a sustained increase in each species' growth, −J⁻¹. Returns (sign, determinacy, stable): the most common sign of each response (row: responding species, column: pressed species; 0 when the response is structurally zero, below 1e-10 of the largest), the fraction of stable draws with that outcome, and the fraction of draws that were stable.

From time series

EcologicalResilience.smap_jacobians — Function
smap_jacobians(X; theta = 2.0, ridge = 0.0, dt = 1.0, times = 1:size(X, 1) - 1,
               continuous = true, meta = MatrixMetadata())

Local Jacobians from a multivariate time series X (rows are times at spacing dt, columns species) by the S-map. For each target time t*, every xᵢ(t + 1) is regressed linearly on x(t) (with intercept), weighting each past state by exp(−theta d / d̄), where d is its distance from x(t*) and d̄ the mean distance; ridge > 0 adds an L2 penalty on the slopes. The slopes form the one-step map Jacobian Φ(t*). With continuous = true it is converted to a continuous-time Jacobian by log(Φ)/dt (real part); otherwise Φ is returned in a Jacobian (map) and only its spectral radius is meaningful for stability. theta = 0 gives one global linear (VAR(1)) fit. Returns a vector of Jacobians, one per element of times, each with the state x(t*) as its equilibrium field (a reference state, not an equilibrium).

source
EcologicalResilience.invariability — Function
invariability(x)

Temporal invariability mean(x) / std(x) of a series (Haegeman et al. 2016), e.g. of total community biomass; the data counterpart of stochastic invariability.

Empirical data

Each reader records its conversions in the matrices' metadata. The data and their provenance are described in lab/data/*/README.md.

EcologicalResilience.read_kinlock — Function
read_kinlock(path; control = :monoculture, complete = true)

Plant interaction networks of Kinlock (2019, Dryad doi:10.5061/dryad.1sm06sp, CC0): for each study, a matrix of relative interaction indices (RII ∈ [−1, 1]; entry (i, j) the effect of species j on species i, negative for competition), against monoculture controls (control = :monoculture, column NetworkMonoCtrl, diagonal 0 by construction) or plants grown alone (:alone, NetworkTrueCtrl). With complete = true only networks without missing entries are returned. Returns a vector of (study, species, rii, habitat).

source
EcologicalResilience.alpha_from_rii — Function
alpha_from_rii(net; scale = 1.0)

An AlphaMatrix from a Kinlock network: off-diagonal per-capita effects scale × RII relative to self-regulation −1. RII values are relative changes in performance, not per-capita coefficients, so this is an assumption, recorded in the metadata; the growth rates are unknown and set to 1, which affects equilibria but not the feasibility domain.

source
EcologicalResilience.read_adler — Function
read_adler(path; normalise = true)

Complete competition-coefficient matrices from the database of Adler et al. (2018, Ecology Letters, Dryad doi:10.5061/dryad.q5mg97b, CC0): rows grouped by study, response variable and treatment, kept when every focal × competitor pair (including intraspecific) is present. Signs are put in the Lotka–Volterra convention (negative = competition) using the column "Negative coefs mean competition" (Yes/No; other entries skip the group). With normalise = true each row is divided by the absolute intraspecific coefficient, so the diagonal is −1. Growth rates are not in the database (set to 1). Returns a vector of (study, response, treatment, alpha::AlphaMatrix).

source
EcologicalResilience.read_stein — Function
read_stein(dir)

The gut-microbiota generalised Lotka–Volterra model and data of Stein et al. (2013, PLoS Comput Biol 9:e1003388): dir holds derived/stein2013_glv.csv (the published fit, sheet MmuE of their Dataset S1) and clv_data_stein/raw_data.csv (the time series, MIT-licensed copy from the cLV repository). Returns (alpha, susceptibility, data): the published AlphaMatrix (rows and columns in the sheet's order), the clindamycin susceptibilities ε (a pulse u(t) adds εᵢ u(t) to the per-capita growth of species i), and a long DataFrame with columns mouse, group, time, genus, abundance (10¹¹ 16S copies per cm³) and clindamycin.

ObservedWeb

source
EcologicalResilience.read_gateway — Function
read_gateway(path; detritus = :basal)

The food webs of GATEWAy v1.0 from its link table (283_2_FoodWebDataBase_2018_12_10.csv). Names are stripped of surrounding spaces and exact duplicate rows dropped. A node's mass is the geometric mean of the masses recorded for it (masses are per link and life stage; −999 means missing). Non-living resources (metabolic type detritus, dead organic material or other) are kept as basal nodes (detritus = :basal) or removed with their links (:drop). Returns a vector of ObservedWeb.

source
EcologicalResilience.trophic_species — Function
trophic_species(w::ObservedWeb)

Aggregate taxa with identical prey and identical predators (and the same living status) into trophic species, the unit with which Williams & Martinez (2000) compared the niche model with observed webs. Repeat until no two nodes share both sets (merging can make further nodes identical). A trophic species' name joins its taxa with " | ", its mass is the geometric mean of its taxa's recorded masses, and its metabolic type is that of its first taxon.

In the bioenergetic model, taxa with the same links and trophic level get identical parameters. If two of them survive, the equilibrium is neutral (the Jacobian has an eigenvalue 0), so observed webs must be aggregated before computing the stability metrics.

source
EcologicalResilience.relative_masses — Function
relative_masses(w::ObservedWeb; Z = 1.5)

Consumer masses relative to the producers, for FoodWebModel(...; mass): divided by the geometric mean mass of the basal living nodes with a recorded mass, or, if none has one, by the smallest consumer mass divided by Z (so the lightest consumer sits one niche-model trophic step above the producers). Returns nothing if some consumer has no mass.

Symbolic models

EcologicalResilience.reaction_network — Function
reaction_network(model::FoodWebModel, B = nothing; name = :foodweb)

The living part of model as a Catalyst ReactionSystem (requires using Catalyst). There is one species Bᵢ per living population, and the reactions are:

  • ∅ → Bᵢ for plant growth;
  • Bᵢ → ∅ for metabolic and natural losses, with a symbolic mortality multiplier μᵢ;
  • Bⱼ → eⱼ B_c for consumer c feeding on j, with the full functional response as its rate, so the assimilation efficiency is a stoichiometry.

q and K are symbolic too. Returns (network, species, mortality, u0, parameters), where u0 maps species to B (if given) and parameters maps parameters to the model's values. Its ODEs are those of rhs!, and its symbolic Jacobian equals jacobian!.
